Dining

Comfort, served warm.

After a walk through damp village lanes, the scent of roasted spices feels especially welcoming. We cook a short menu, twice a day, from whatever the boats and the garden give us.

The lunch plate, most days

The table

Meals to linger over while another shower moves through the garden.

Crisp rava‑fried fish arrives hot, followed by steaming fish curry sharpened with sour kokum. Warm poi bread is torn and passed around the table.

Conversation stretches, a second helping follows, and an ordinary lunch becomes one of the moments you remember most.

Monsoon menu

Short, and it changes.

Written on a board each morning. If the sea was rough, we cook vegetables and say so.

  • Rava‑fried mackerel

    Semolina crust, chilli and turmeric, fried to order. The dish most guests order twice.

  • Goan fish curry

    Coconut, red chilli and sour kokum, with whatever came off the boat that morning.

  • Prawn balchão

    Sharp, dark and slow‑cooked. Best with plain rice and patience.

  • Mushroom xacuti

    Roasted coconut and a long list of spices. Our monsoon vegetarian standard.

  • Poi, warm

    Baked in the village, delivered twice a day, torn at the table.

  • Solkadhi

    Pink, cool and kokum‑sour. Order it with everything.

  • Lemongrass chai

    Jaggery, ginger, lemongrass from the courtyard. Poured on the veranda from seven.

  • Bebinca

    Layered, slow, unreasonably rich. One slice between two, in theory.
